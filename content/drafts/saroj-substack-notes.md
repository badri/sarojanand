# Saroj Anand — Substack Notes (32)

Batch of notes for scheduling. Voice: curious, specific, opinionated. Engineer who reads too much and connects unlikely things. Never meta about writing. Always about the world.

---

The Kailasa Temple at Ellora was carved top-down from a single rock. They removed 200,000 tonnes of basalt — roughly 40,000 truckloads. With no power tools. No cranes. We still don't fully understand the quarrying sequence. Aliens? No. Engineers we forgot about? Almost certainly.

---

In 1981, a group of 400 income tax officers in India disguised themselves as a wedding procession — complete with a band, garlands, and a fake groom on a horse — to raid a hawala operator's mansion during an actual wedding next door. This is better than any heist movie ever made.

---

The Dark Forest hypothesis says the universe is silent because every civilization is hiding. If you announce yourself, someone more advanced destroys you. It's game theory applied to the cosmos. Also describes most corporate Slack channels.

---

A Tamil man named Ravi ran IBomma — a movie piracy site with billions of views — from a small town in Telangana. His operation made ₹20 crores. Meanwhile, Israel's Unit 8200 trains teenagers to hack nation-states. Same skill, different zip code. The 21st century in one sentence.

---

The Silurian Hypothesis asks: if an industrial civilization existed 50 million years ago, what evidence would survive? The answer is almost nothing. Plastics decompose. Metals corrode. Concrete crumbles. Only a thin chemical anomaly in rock strata. We could be the second civilization on this planet and never know.

---

Puranic cosmology describes cyclical time, simulated realities (Maya), and consciousness as the substrate of existence. Modern simulation theory is basically Advaita Vedanta with GPUs. We spent $200 billion to arrive where Adi Shankara was in 800 AD.

---

Undersea internet cables carry 97% of intercontinental data. When one breaks near the Andaman Islands, a team of divers goes down to splice fiber optics at 2,000 meters depth. Your Netflix works because someone is doing surgery on the ocean floor right now.

---

mtDNA traces show that every human alive descends from a single woman in East Africa about 200,000 years ago. The first humans to reach India arrived roughly 65,000 years ago, walking along the coast. They didn't know they were colonizing. They were just following the fish.

---

In 2023, scientists grew a mini-brain in a lab — a cluster of neurons in a dish. It learned to play Pong. Not well, but it learned. Nobody is asking the important question: did it enjoy winning?

---

Mount Kailash has never been climbed. Not because it's technically impossible — it's only 6,638 meters. It's because every country with jurisdiction considers it too sacred. A mountain protected by belief rather than altitude. That's more interesting than any summit.

---

There's an underground cave system beneath the Kugai Murugan temple in Tamil Nadu that nobody has fully mapped. The tunnels go deeper than the temple's recorded history. Local priests say there are chambers that "breathe." Engineers say it's thermal convection. Both might be right.

---

Anatoly Bugorski stuck his head into a running particle accelerator in 1978. The proton beam burned through his skull at nearly the speed of light. He survived. Lost hearing in one ear, half his face is paralyzed, but his brain works fine. Sometimes the universe just decides not to kill you.

---

A CBI officer I read about once said the hardest part of a raid isn't the planning. It's the 20 minutes before you go in, when you're sitting in a van eating vada pav, pretending everything is normal, while your hands shake. Crime fiction never gets that part right.

---

Chettinad mansions in Tamil Nadu have 50+ rooms, Burma teak pillars, Italian marble floors, and hand-painted tiles from Japan. Most were built by merchants who made fortunes in Southeast Asia in the 1800s. Today, most stand empty. A house remembers wealth differently than a person does.

---

The Fermi Paradox isn't really about aliens. It's about our inability to tolerate silence. We built telescopes, sent golden records into space, beamed signals at stars — all because we cannot accept that no one is listening. The loneliest species in the universe keeps shouting into the void.

---

If you live in India and want to understand geopolitics, study the Indian Ocean. Every submarine route, every cable landing station, every chokepoint from Malacca to Hormuz — that's where the next war will be decided. Not on land. Not in cyberspace. In water.

---

In Sujatha Rangarajan's novels, the smartest character is always an engineer who reads too much. He's never the hero. He's the one who figures out what happened three chapters before anyone else, then has to decide whether to tell anyone. I've never related to a character archetype more.

---

The Betz Sphere: in 1974, a family in Florida found a perfectly polished metal sphere in their yard after a fire. It moved on its own. It resonated when struck with specific frequencies. The Navy examined it and returned it without explanation. It's probably mundane. But "probably mundane" is the scariest phrase in any mystery.

---

Zambia had a space program in 1964. A schoolteacher named Edward Makuka Nkoloso trained his "astronauts" by rolling them downhill in oil drums and swinging them on ropes. He planned to reach Mars. He was laughed at. But consider: he imagined an African space program 60 years before anyone else took the idea seriously.

---

A neurosurgeon in Australia pulled a live 8-centimeter worm from a woman's brain. It was a python parasite that had never been found in a human before. The woman had been picking native grasses near a lake. Nature doesn't care about your food chain assumptions.

---

The Nattukottai Chettiars ran one of the most sophisticated banking networks in history — from small towns in Tamil Nadu to Rangoon, Singapore, Colombo, and Saigon. No formal training. No degrees. Just ledgers, trust networks, and compound interest calculated by hand. Every fintech founder should study them first.

---

Every thriller I love has the same structure underneath: a competent person notices something small, follows it, and discovers the small thing is connected to something enormous. The gap between "that's odd" and "oh no" is where all good stories live.

---

Visakhapatnam has one of India's most secret military installations — a nuclear submarine base built inside a mountain. The entrance is underwater. Satellites can't see the subs entering or leaving. This is not fiction. This is Tuesday in the Indian Navy.

---

In the Mahabharata, Arjuna is given a weapon called the Pashupatastra that can destroy all of creation. The condition: it must never be used against a lesser opponent, or it will destroy the wielder instead. This is the best description of nuclear deterrence I've ever read, written 2,000 years early.

---

I grew up in a house where my grandmother knew the exact raga for every mood, every time of day, every season. Morning? Bilahari. Evening? Kalyani. Sadness? Sahana. She had an algorithm for emotion long before Spotify existed. Hers was better.

---

Time travel stories always focus on the big changes — kill Hitler, prevent disasters. But real time travel would break you with small things. The smell of your grandmother's kitchen in 1987. Your father's voice before it got tired. Nobody writes about the time traveler who goes back and just... sits there.

---

The Terracotta Warriors were discovered by farmers digging a well. 8,000 life-sized soldiers buried for 2,200 years. Every face is unique. Someone sculpted 8,000 individual human faces, knowing they would be buried in the dark forever. That's not military planning. That's obsession.

---

A hypothesis I can't stop thinking about: what if the reason we haven't found alien signals is that advanced civilizations go quiet on purpose? Not because they're hiding (Dark Forest), but because they've figured out that broadcasting is simply... rude? The universe as a library, not a party.

---

The best thing about Carnatic music is that every performance is both completely structured and completely improvised. The raga sets the rules. The musician breaks them. The audience knows exactly when. It's controlled chaos with a 2,000-year-old operating system.

---

When I was twelve, my physics teacher told us that the atoms in our body were forged inside dying stars. I thought he was being poetic. He wasn't. Your left hand and your right hand probably contain atoms from different stars. You are, literally, a collaboration between dead suns.

---

The biggest bank heist in history wasn't a vault job. It was a SWIFT network hack. Bangladesh Bank, 2016. North Korean hackers stole $81 million by sending fake transfer requests during a weekend. The only reason they didn't get $951 million was a spelling error in one transaction. Crime is now a software problem.

---

There's a village in Tamil Nadu where the annual temple festival involves carrying burning pots on your head while running through the streets at 3 AM. No one gets hurt. No one questions it. If you grew up there, this is just Thursday. If you didn't, it's the opening scene of a novel.

---

The oldest continuously inhabited city in the world is probably Varanasi. People have been living there, dying there, burning their dead on the same ghats, for roughly 5,000 years. The city doesn't care about your timeline. It was here before you and it will be here after. Everything else is just visiting.
